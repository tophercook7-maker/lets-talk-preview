# Google Business Profile — now legitimate, wasn't before

**What changed (2026-09-07):** asked whether she'd ever meet someone in person, she said
**yes**. That is the whole test. Google requires either a place customers visit or a business
that travels to the customer; a purely-online business does not qualify and gets suspended.
She qualifies as a **service-area business** in Hot Springs.

⚠️ This one she can actually share properly — unlike Cal.com free, **GBP has real Manager
roles**. She creates and verifies it, then adds Topher as a Manager. Nobody passes a password.

---

## Only she can do this (~15 min, then a wait)

1. **google.com/business** → Manage now, signed in as **her own Google account**
   *(the same one the site's calendar will use — keeps it all in one place)*
2. **Business name: `Let's Talk with Deanna`**
   ⚠️ Exactly the real-world name. No "coach Hot Springs" keyword stuffing — that is the
   single most common reason these get suspended.
3. **Category** — primary: the closest of *Business management consultant* / *Business
   consultant* / *Life coach*. Pick one that honestly describes her; add a second if it fits.
4. **"Do you have a location customers can visit?" → No.** That makes it a service-area
   business: her address is collected but **never shown publicly**.
5. **Service area**: Hot Springs, plus Hot Springs Village / Benton / Malvern if she's willing
   to drive there. Don't claim the whole state.
6. **Phone**: her Google Voice number (set that up first — RUNBOOK §4). Not her personal cell.
7. **Website**: `https://lets-talk2.me`
8. **Verification** — Google picks the method; for service-area businesses it's usually a
   **video** these days (she records her surroundings and something showing the business is
   hers), sometimes a postcard. Either way it needs her, and it can take a few days.
9. Once verified: **Users → Add → Topher as Manager.** Then he does the rest.

---

## Then Topher fills it in
- **Description** (750 chars): her 20+ years, what she actually helps with, plain language
- **Services**: the three session lengths and the monthly, with the real prices
- **Hours**: her genuine availability, matching Cal.com — mismatched hours annoy people
- **Photos**: her real photo plus the site's imagery. ⚠️ Google strips obviously synthetic
  images and it reads as spammy — lead with the real one.
- **Messaging**: leave OFF unless she wants a fourth inbox. She already has chat on the site.
- **Review link**: grab the short `g.page` link once live → that's the review flow in RUNBOOK §6

---

## ⚠️ Two things to settle before creating it

**1. The site doesn't mention in-person anywhere.** It currently says *"Wherever you are."*
If the GBP claims a Hot Springs service area while the site reads as online-only, that
inconsistency is exactly what a suspension review looks at. Either add a line to the site
acknowledging she'll meet locally, or don't create the GBP. Right now the site's structured
data lists Hot Springs alongside Worldwide, which is honest but thin on its own.

**2. She'd be meeting strangers from the internet, one-on-one.** Worth one conversation
before it's advertised: public places only, and she tells someone where she'll be. Not a
reason to skip it — just not something to discover on the first booking.

## ♻️ Reusable
The in-person question is the gate for every client in this package. Ask it early — the answer
decides whether GBP is on the table at all, and GBP is the single highest-value free listing
for a local service business. For genuinely online-only clients, RUNBOOK §5 has the
alternatives (LinkedIn, coaching directories, the site's own SEO, referrals).
